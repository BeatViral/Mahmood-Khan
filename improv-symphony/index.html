The idea

What happens when a symphony orchestra is invited not only to perform a composition, but to respond, converse and improvise within it?

Improv Symphony is a musical concept created by Mahmood Khan. The original song remains the anchor. Around it, musicians can listen, respond and discover something spontaneous together. The performance becomes a living conversation rather than a completely predetermined arrangement.

Where it comes from

Mahmood's musical journey began in studios where American funk was a mainstay. He became fascinated by holding a small set of chords in a repeating groove, then letting musicians improvise and create something spontaneous around that musical foundation.

Later, while studying under Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan and exploring Pakistani qawwali, he encountered the same spirit of improvisation in a very different musical tradition: the freedom to listen, answer, extend and transform a shared musical idea.

Improv Symphony takes that experience into the orchestral imagination. It is not simply an orchestra placed behind a pop song. It asks how the orchestra itself might take part in the creative exchange.

The semicircle: the musicians as one conversation

In Mahmood's bands, musicians sit together in a U-shaped semicircle so they can see and respond to one another. Mahmood does not stand apart as a frontman with musicians behind him. Eye contact, listening and nonverbal cues are part of the musical arrangement itself.

The exact orchestral configuration will be developed to suit the ensemble, conductor and venue. The essential idea is the same: communication should remain alive among the musicians.

What happens when an orchestra joins the conversation?

Imagine musicians working within a simple shared framework — an original melody, harmonic loop, groove or musical phrase — while creating space for orchestral sections and individual instruments to listen, answer, develop and surprise one another.

The song preserves its identity; the orchestral performance is free to evolve. The score may guide its framework, but not every phrase or interaction has to be written in advance.

Possible musical elements to explore:

  • Original song as foundation: the core melody, structure and emotional identity remain recognisable.
  • Groove and repetition: a rhythmic or harmonic foundation provides a place from which to improvise.
  • Musical exchange: sections and musicians respond to each other's phrases and gestures.
  • Collective uplift: the ensemble builds shared energy and discovers a new passage in real time.
  • Return: a recognisable musical point brings the audience back to the song.

These are exploratory musical principles, not a finished compositional formula.

An orchestral history — and a new direction

Mahmood's earlier collaboration with the Willoughby Symphony Orchestra reached No. 1 on Billboard's Classical Crossover Albums chart. It shows his existing history of bringing original songs into orchestral settings. Improv Symphony represents a new direction from that orchestral history.

A living international idea

Through Mahmood Khan with Orchestras of the World, the same original songs could travel between orchestras. Each performance would reflect the musicians, musical traditions and artistic character of its place, generating unique concerts, recordings and films.

Improv Symphony is intended to evolve through performance. It may also become the artistic foundation for recurring annual concert events, allowing the musical language and its audience to grow over time.
