Mahmood Khan

Singer-songwriter, musician, producer, engineer and filmmaker — an artist whose work connects Pakistan, Los Angeles, India and Australia.

This is the complete source-led archive of Mahmood Khan’s story, releases, collaborations, chart achievements, recognition and press record. Claims are presented as covered by the linked articles and archive sources below.

The complete record

From Lahore to a world-pop voice

Mahmood Khan was born in Lahore, Pakistan, on 4 August 1967, to Matloob ur Rahman and Durdana Matloob. His family’s story, like much of the subcontinent’s modern history, was shaped by migration after 1947. Khan’s early musical life took in piano and guitar, school performances at Sadiq Public School in Bahawalpur, and later study in the United States.

In 1985 he moved to Los Angeles and spent around twelve years with Jam Power, working across R&B, jazz, hip-hop and pop as an engineer, producer and songwriter. The period connected him with artists and producers including Chuckii Booker, Mary J. Blige, Eazy-E, N.W.A., Vanessa Williams and Coolio. His work on Coolio’s Gangsta’s Paradise and Vanessa Williams’ The Right Stuff is discussed in the AudioBoom interview.

Khan’s recording catalogue includes Fairytale (1995), Only One (1997), Alive, Panah featuring Nandini Srikar, and Made in Australia. Only One includes the song “Anarkali” with Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan; later coverage recounts Nusrat’s encouragement and prediction for Khan’s future.

Core identity

Pakistani-Australian singer-songwriter, music producer, sound engineer and filmmaker.

His sound is often described as world pop: Western pop, folk, jazz and South Asian melodic traditions meeting orchestral, electronic and acoustic production.

Pakistan, India and Australia

Khan performed at the South Asian Games in 2004 and worked in Mumbai through Magnasound. After making Australia his home, he recorded “Like the River” live at the Sydney Opera House. Coverage credits the song as the first by a Pakistani artist to reach No. 1 on the ARIA Pop Charts, and notes a Channel Nine Kerri-Anne appearance following the song’s major YouTube audience.

The Willoughby Symphony collaboration

The orchestral project brought together Khan’s songs, arrangements by Doug Emery, conductor David Griffin, the Willoughby Symphony Orchestra and Aboriginal female choir Gambirra Mob. The recordings included “Jagamarra/Eternity Return,” named for Khan’s first Aboriginal friend in Australia. Willoughby Mayor Gail Giles-Gidney, Desert Song Festival, C31 Melbourne and Channel 44 Adelaide were among those cited in coverage of the collaboration.

Early releases and collaborators

A studio life across continents

Made in Australia credits collaborators including Doug Emery, Lee Levin, Ed Calle, Julio Hernandez and Dan Warner. Across releases and production work, the archive identifies collaboration or association with Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, Dick Marx, Damon Thomas, Gary Grant, Julio Hernandez, Eazy-E, Leon Sylvers, Ed Calle, Lee Levin, Dan Warner, Chuckii Booker, Shibani Kashyap, Nandini Srikar and Stevie B.

Song titles documented through the archive include “Runaway,” “One Line Down,” “Ginoo,” “Merry Go Round,” “Tere Baghair,” “Like the River,” “Jagamarra/Eternity Return,” “Agar,” “Blue Snow,” and “Wings.”

Biography / retained archive chapters

Early life

Raised in Pakistan, Khan developed his ear through piano, guitar, melody and performance before studying in the United States. The archive records his time at Sadiq Public School, Bahawalpur and the University of North Carolina at Charlotte as formative parts of an education that travelled alongside music.

Los Angeles — the studio

Los Angeles gave Khan an extended studio apprenticeship. From 1985 he worked with Jam Power for around twelve years, learning the craft of engineering, songwriting and production while moving through R&B, jazz, hip-hop and pop. The work and networks formed there remain part of the recorded story behind his later world-pop catalogue.

Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan

The meeting of Mahmood Khan and Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan is central to the archive. Their collaboration on “Anarkali” for the 1997 album Only One brought Khan’s songwriting together with Nusrat’s vocal tradition; the linked legacy coverage recounts Nusrat’s belief in Khan’s future.

Writing across places

Khan’s writing has continued across Pakistan, India, the United States and Australia — moving naturally between Urdu and English songs, acoustic and electronic textures, and intimate songs and symphonic scale.

Sydney Opera House

“Like the River,” recorded live at the Sydney Opera House, is a defining Australian chapter in the archive. Coverage associates its reported ARIA Pop-chart result and broad YouTube audience with Khan’s Channel Nine Kerri-Anne appearance.

The orchestral project

The Willoughby Symphony project united Mahmood Khan’s songs with Doug Emery’s score, David Griffin’s conducting, the orchestra and Gambirra Mob. It was Khan’s first professional symphony collaboration and a creative bridge between melody, orchestra and multicultural voices.

Recognition reported in 2020

The 2020–21 press record documents an intense run of iTunes and Billboard placements across “Runaway,” “One Line Down,” “Ginoo,” “Merry Go Round” and “Tere Baghair,” alongside the Australian Government Distinguished Talent recognition recorded on this page.

Chart achievements

International chart record

The following placements are documented in the linked press coverage and artist archive. They are retained here as a consolidated record of the reported achievements.

  • Willoughby Symphony Orchestra project: reported No. 1 on Billboard Classical Crossover and No. 1 on iTunes in the United States, Australia, France, Switzerland, Mexico and Turkey.
  • Imaginary Friend: reported No. 1 on US iTunes Jazz, after first entering at No. 5; the album was also reported at No. 1 on US iTunes.
  • Jahan Tu Nahin: reported at No. 18 on Billboard World Music, with coverage also citing a No. 12 Billboard position.
  • Ginoo: reported at No. 4 on Billboard; the archive describes it as the first Urdu song to crack Billboard.
  • Jagamarra / Sydney: reported Top 10 US iTunes Classical placements.
  • Blue Snow: reported No. 7; Innocence No. 15; Battle Is Uphill No. 5; Little Bed No. 4.
  • April 2021 run: “Ginoo” No. 4, “One Line Down” No. 5, “Tere Ooper Chaon” No. 6, “Din Jagay” No. 7, “Merry Go Round (ukulele)” No. 8, and “Merry Go Round” No. 9.
  • Tere Baghair: reported No. 1 on US iTunes on 6 June 2020.
  • Wings: reported No. 9, a tribute to Dan Warner.

Featured release / 2022

Rarity breaks records

Rarity is documented as a six-track EP: “Automation,” “Battle Is Uphill,” “Chal Dil Mere,” “Meaning of Life,” “No Feelin When You’re Gone,” and “Rarity.” The IssueWire feature reports “Battle Is Uphill” in the US iTunes Top Ten, along with the orchestral project’s international No. 1 run and eight songs in the USA Top 100 in April 2021.

Single spotlight

Blue Snow

Daily Times coverage follows “Blue Snow” onto international music charts and records its reported No. 7 result. The song also appears in the Ballina live-set coverage as part of Khan’s world-pop performance repertoire.

Jazz collection / 2021

Imaginary Friend

The five-track Imaginary Friend collection was covered as a genre-mixing jazz release. The report notes a No. 5 US iTunes Jazz entry before a reported No. 1 position, six songs in the US Top 100 in April 2021, and further multi-country chart success with the Willoughby Symphony project.

Government recognition

Distinguished Talent

Mahmood Khan was recognised as a Distinguished Talent by the Australian Government for services rendered to the expansion of the Australian music industry.

Live / Ballina 2022

World pop fruits in Ballina

The Ballina feature describes a Resonator Music performance with a six-piece band, presenting Khan’s World/Pop sound through “Ginoo,” “Agar,” “Merry Go Round,” “One Line Down” and “Blue Snow.” It also references the documentary Journey of a New Sound.

Legacy / Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan

A prediction comes true

Daily Times traces Khan’s connection with Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan through Only One (1997), and revisits the wider career arc: Los Angeles production work, Black funk and hip-hop influences, commercial reach for Only One, US radio and MTV Asia, the Channel Nine appearance following a reported 50 million YouTube views, and Khan’s memoir Stayin Alive.
