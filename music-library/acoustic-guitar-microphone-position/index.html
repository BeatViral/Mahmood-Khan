How to find an acoustic guitar's microphone sweet spot

Acoustic instruments radiate different frequencies in different directions, so tiny placement changes matter.

WORLD POP music knowledge library · November 2026

AI-assisted research note · Sources linked below · Not a broadcast transcript or a first-person account by Mahmood Khan

The underlying sound

An acoustic guitar is not a single point source. Strings, bridge, soundboard and body contribute different frequencies, and their sound combines as it travels through the room. A microphone directly at the soundhole may capture a different balance from one aimed toward the neck joint or a position farther away. The right choice depends on the player's technique and the arrangement.

What changes in practice

Close miking can emphasise detail and reduce room pickup, while greater distance may create a more integrated sound if the acoustic space is good. Stereo techniques can add width, but mismatched arrival times can lead to phase problems when the channels are combined.

Try it in the studio

Record the same short guitar phrase with the microphone near the soundhole, near the twelfth fret and farther from the instrument. Match the level in playback and compare bass weight, pick noise and body resonance. Check the sound in mono before deciding on stereo microphone combinations.

The decision that matters

Do not search for one universally correct position. A fingerpicked part beneath a delicate vocal may need a different tone from a rhythmic acoustic part driving an energetic song.
