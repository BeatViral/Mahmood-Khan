Before WORLD POP: the 2010 experiment in broadcasting music and studio life

WORLD POP's combination of songs, musical explanation and personal experience has a documented earlier chapter.

WORLD POP music knowledge library · November 2026

AI-assisted research note · Sources linked below · Not a broadcast transcript or a first-person account by Mahmood Khan

An older idea with a recognisable ambition

In April 2010, Mahmood Khan's own archive announced a plan for a weekly internet programme to be broadcast from recording studios, homes and places with a connection to his musical life. The opening was described as taking place at Studios 301 in Sydney. The proposed programme was about more than playing songs; it would give audiences access to the places and people involved in making them.

A separate 2010 promotional announcement refers to a programme titled Journey of a New Sound on Livestream. It describes unplugged music, discussions with artists and coverage of recording, mixing and production. These announcements show what was envisaged at the time; without a complete recording archive we should not assume every proposed broadcast took place.

The documentary shares a title, but is a different form

A documentary also called Journey of a New Sound was listed in the artist's November 2011 archive and credited to Hungarian filmmaker Zsolt Nagy. The documentary listing, press coverage and proposed Livestream show are different sources and should not be merged into a single production without checking the original video and broadcast dates.

That distinction matters for a reliable historical archive. Similar titles can refer to an ongoing creative theme while describing different media formats: a live programme, a documentary, a recording session or a television special.

What WORLD POP develops from that impulse

WORLD POP with Mahmood Khan is now presented as a weekly programme about the songs, stories and cultures behind music. The continuity is clear at the level of artistic curiosity: the host wants listeners to hear the work and understand the lives, techniques and environments that shaped it.

The newer programme has a more defined editorial form and published episode archive. Its recorded conversations and musical stories can be indexed accurately and used to connect the present to the older interviews and footage.

A test for the next stage of the library

An archive should preserve what really happened, with links to audio and video wherever available. A promotional announcement belongs in the history, but it is not proof that the corresponding episode survives. For an original programme, an accurate transcript and dated recording are stronger than a collection of paraphrased claims.

The opportunity is to connect every verified WORLD POP episode to older documented experiences without pretending that all those experiences have already been recounted on-air. That combination gives listeners context, while giving future researchers an honest trail back to the original sources.
