What is an earworm, and why does a chorus return uninvited?

An earworm is involuntary musical imagery, not proof that a song is universally memorable.

WORLD POP music knowledge library · November 2026

AI-assisted research note · Sources linked below · Not a broadcast transcript or a first-person account by Mahmood Khan

The question

Researchers use the term involuntary musical imagery for hearing music in one's mind without deliberately starting it. The familiar word is earworm. Studies describe short fragments, often with lyrics and commonly from choruses, recurring during everyday activities. It is a normal feature of musical imagination, although individuals differ in how frequently it happens.

What to listen for

Catchiness is not an exact equation. Familiarity, recent exposure, musical structure and the listener's circumstances all contribute. A hook that is instantly identifiable may be easier to remember, but studies do not support a universal recipe for creating the world's next hit. In particular, a repeated mental fragment is not the same as affection for the whole recording.

Try this

Over a few days, record which song fragment appears unexpectedly, whether you heard the record recently and where the fragment begins and ends. Compare that fragment with the full song. The exercise reveals how much our memories compress music into recognisable gestures.

A useful boundary

For songwriters, this is a useful way to examine a chorus without treating brain science as a marketing guarantee. Ask what the listener can sing back after one hearing, then ask what makes them want to hear the entire song again.
