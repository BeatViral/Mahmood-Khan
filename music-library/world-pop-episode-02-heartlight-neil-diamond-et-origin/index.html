Heartlight: the film that inspired Neil Diamond, Bacharach and Carole Bayer Sager

Heartlight was not an official commission for E.T.; Neil Diamond's own recollection explains how a cinema visit became a new song.

WORLD POP music knowledge library · November 2026

AI-assisted research note · Sources linked below · Not a broadcast transcript or a first-person account by Mahmood Khan

A movie experience became a pop song

Neil Diamond's 'Heartlight' is one of the key songs in Mahmood Khan's Episode 2 sequence connecting Burt Bacharach, Carole Bayer Sager and David Foster. The song's origin is easily misunderstood: it is associated with E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial, but it was not commissioned as part of that film's soundtrack.

Diamond explained the story in a retrospective interview with Parade. He, Bacharach and Sager went to see the film in New York because everyone was discussing it. After the screening, he suggested writing a song inspired by what they had experienced. He later recalled hearing that Steven Spielberg liked the finished recording, although the song arrived too late to be placed in the movie.

Three writers, different musical histories

The writers combined Diamond's distinctive performing identity with Bacharach's melodic and arranging sensibility and Bayer Sager's long experience in song composition. The official Bacharach press biography also acknowledges the E.T. inspiration. The recording is often presented as a single artist's hit, but the authorship tells a more collaborative story.

This is exactly the sort of connection WORLD POP can expose. Listeners recognise Neil Diamond's voice first. Learning who shared the writing can change the way they hear the structure, melodic contours and relationship between voice and accompaniment, without pretending to know which writer proposed each note or lyric.

The recording has another important collaborator

Carole Bayer Sager's own collaborator archive credits David Foster with creating a memorable figure heard at the start of 'Heartlight'. That first-person account is more informative than simply listing him among the session musicians. A brief instrumental gesture can help establish the song's sound-world before the vocal enters.

A songwriter's memory of a colleague provides valuable context, though it does not mean that every subsequent instrumental note came from that colleague. The finished recording is a collective object: composition, performance, arrangement, engineering and production all affect how the audience receives its emotional centre.

What this tells us about inspiration

A film can inspire a piece of music without becoming part of its soundtrack. Creative influence is not identical to a formal commission or a shared catalogue credit. The 'Heartlight' story demonstrates artists responding to an experience and giving it a new musical form in another medium.

A listener might first hear the song without knowing any of this. The deeper account does not have to replace that private response. It offers another layer: a cinema visit in 1982, a conversation among three songwriters, Foster's instrumental contribution and Diamond's eventual recording. The Episode 2 script connects the record to other composers; the original interviews supply this history.
