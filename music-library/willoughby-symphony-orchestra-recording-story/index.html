Inside the making of Mahmood Khan's Willoughby Symphony recordings

The orchestra project combined songwriting, arranging, recording, filming and a different way of presenting musical identity.

WORLD POP music knowledge library · November 2026

AI-assisted research note · Sources linked below · Not a broadcast transcript or a first-person account by Mahmood Khan

Why a full symphony mattered

In an August 2020 Daily Times interview, Mahmood Khan said he had wanted for years to record his original songs with a large symphony orchestra. He considered a smaller chamber ensemble but wanted the scale and range of a full symphonic sound. He described developing the repertoire over an extended period, with arrangements by Doug Emery and David Griffin.

This was a choice about sound and artistic identity, not only prestige. A larger ensemble offers ways to sustain harmonies, layer counter-melodies, reshape dynamics and build intensity that a conventional pop band does differently.

The artist was managing several kinds of production

Khan brought filmmaking training into the recording project and described hiring a seven-person camera crew and a director of photography. In the studio, his attention moved between discussing camera placement and lighting, consulting on the microphone and recording plan, and performing the lead vocals.

That account makes the project an interesting case study in multidisciplinary authorship. The musician was not only the person in front of the microphones; he was also considering how the sound and its creation would be documented visually.

Finishing what was recorded

The interview describes a substantial post-production period of mixing, mastering and editing video after the orchestra session. Such work is often invisible to someone who only encounters the finished song. An ensemble recording contains many performances at once, and making them clear requires a sustained process of listening and judgement.

Khan's association with Willoughby Symphony Orchestra resulted in a Billboard No. 1 on the Classical Crossover Albums chart. The category is specific, and the achievement should always be identified precisely rather than represented as an overall Billboard chart No. 1.

From one orchestral session to a wider creative question

The recording illustrates a long-standing interest in how original popular songs can inhabit an orchestral world. That question now connects naturally to Khan's exploration of orchestral improvisation, though the established Willoughby session and the later Improv Symphony concept are distinct projects.

A richer archive could add the arrangement credits, dated rehearsal and recording footage, full personnel, and commentary from orchestral musicians. The best story would not be a single triumph narrative but a detailed account of what individual musicians contributed to a new sound.
