Microphone hours: the vocal advice Mahmood Khan gave Karen

Practice, playback and guidance: a practical three-part answer to a singer-songwriter's question about voice and confidence.

WORLD POP music knowledge library · November 2026

AI-assisted research note · Sources linked below · Not a broadcast transcript or a first-person account by Mahmood Khan

The listener question turns the programme toward practice

In the Episode 2 WORLD POP planning conversation, Mahmood Khan worked on a reply to Karen, a singer-songwriter seeking guidance about her voice. His central metaphor is 'microphone hours': just as a pilot logs flying hours, a developing singer learns from spending time recording and listening.

The advice is not to buy new equipment before building experience. Khan groups his answer into three habits: regular practice, recording and listening back to yourself, and vocal lessons. Recording gives a singer something concrete to examine: phrasing, breath, confidence, pitch decisions and how clearly a lyric is communicated.

Why hearing your own recorded voice is useful

The difference between performing and listening back can be startling. A singer hears their voice partly through bone conduction while performing; a microphone, room and playback system produce a different perspective. That contrast is one reason a recording may initially sound unfamiliar even to its performer.

Repeated, attentive playback can help people learn what the microphone actually captured, rather than depending only on their impression while singing. This should not become an endless attempt to correct every detail. The practical point in Khan's script is to accumulate experience and use the playback as evidence.

Two teachers, two musical worlds

Khan tells Karen he was fortunate to study with the vocal teacher Seth Riggs and that he also learned important things from Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan. He indicates that he intends to tell both stories more fully in later programmes. This companion article does not invent those promised accounts or assume they were included in full in Episode 2.

Seth Riggs became known for his Speech Level Singing approach, the subject of a long interview with Red Bull Music Academy and professional voice-pedagogy literature. Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan's singing emerged from a very different tradition, rooted in qawwali and South Asian vocal practice. Khan's interest in both illustrates the breadth of his listening experience without treating the techniques as interchangeable.

From a question to a song

The production notes connect Karen's question with a play cue for Khan's original recording 'All We Need to Know'. This is an important editorial step: instead of leaving the discussion as abstract advice, the presenter brings listeners back to an actual performance.

The useful lesson is not a guarantee that a fixed number of hours makes someone a good singer. It's a process: practise, document the sound, listen carefully and seek knowledgeable feedback. Here, those principles come from the user's Episode 2 working script; the recording has not yet been transcribed and compared line by line.
