Journey of a New Sound: the documentary and the surviving source trail

An evidence-led guide to the Zsolt Nagy documentary, its historical listing and the related television-interview archive.

WORLD POP music knowledge library · November 2026

AI-assisted research note · Sources linked below · Not a broadcast transcript or a first-person account by Mahmood Khan

A film named in the original archive

Mahmood Khan's own MKF archive contains a post dated 17 November 2011 identifying a documentary about him directed by Hungarian filmmaker Zsolt Nagy. The site's tags include Journey of a New Sound, Sydney Film School and Sydney Opera House. A later Daily Times article likewise names the filmmaker and documentary and describes its focus on the artist's experience of pressure and achievement in Australia.

These sources are enough to establish that the documentary belongs in the historical record. They are not enough to reproduce its dialogue, scene order or full list of contributors. The original watchable film has not yet been recovered and examined for this edition of the archive.

What the surrounding media record reveals

On the same date, the MKF archive listed a long television interview associated with Mark Olford and a special marking an announced 50-million-view milestone. It also listed Pakistan news-network interviews and television coverage. Those listings point toward a rich historical record of how Khan explained his music and career in different media contexts.

The video sources should be inventoried individually. A YouTube link, original upload title, duration, date and accurate transcript would allow each interview to support specific statements. Until those files are found, we should not present the titles of archive posts as if they were verbatim records of the programmes.

How the documentary could improve this library

A film can preserve details that newspaper interviews compress: the actual tone of a voice, the setting in which a person answers a question, the visual evidence of a rehearsal, and the pauses around a difficult memory. These are reasons to seek and transcribe the original film rather than infer its contents from a synopsis.

Once verified, the material could illuminate chapters already suggested by other interviews: early musical influences, Los Angeles engineering, collaboration with Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, the Sydney Opera House project and the artistic challenges of living and working in Australia. Those are research directions, not a claim that every topic appears in the film.

The next evidence we need

The highest priority is the film's original YouTube or master-file location, followed by an accurate transcription with timestamps and any filmmaker credits that appear on screen. Then the Mark Olford interview and Pakistan television appearances can be approached the same way.

This page is deliberately a source guide rather than a fabricated scene-by-scene analysis. It will become a richer documentary study once the actual recording can be examined and properly credited.
